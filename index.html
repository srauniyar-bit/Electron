Discovery & History

J.J. Thomson (1897)

The electron was discovered by British physicist J.J. Thomson through his experiments with cathode ray tubes. He demonstrated that cathode rays were negatively charged particles, much lighter than atoms.

Cathode Ray Experiment

By applying electric and magnetic fields to the cathode rays in a vacuum tube, Thomson measured the charge-to-mass ratio (e/m) of the electron, proving it was a universal component of all matter.

Millikan's Oil Drop (1909)

Robert Millikan suspended tiny charged oil droplets between two metal electrodes. By balancing gravity and electric forces, he calculated the exact charge of a single electron.

Key Formulas

Specific Charge (e/m)

Thomson's formula for the charge-to-mass ratio using electric (E) and magnetic (B) fields:

e/m = E / (r * B²)

Value: 1.76 × 10¹¹ C/kg

Quantization of Charge

Charge always occurs in integral multiples of fundamental charge.

Q = ±ne

Where n is an integer and e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.

Lorentz Force

The force exerted on an electron moving through an electromagnetic field.

F = -e(E + v × B)

Used in particle accelerators and cyclotrons.
